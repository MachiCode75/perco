# The 13th Bell (test story, original)

In our town, the church bell rings 13 times at midnight. Not 12. 13. Everybody counts. Nobody asks why. Same bell. Same tower. Same 13. Grandpa watches the tower every night through his old binoculars. He says the 13th ring is for whoever didn't make it home. My little sister Lily counts from her bedroom window. When the bell stops, she blinks her flashlight 13 times back at the tower. Like a goodnight. One night, the bell rang 14 times. Lily blinked 14 times. Grandpa lowered his binoculars and didn't say a word. The next morning, Mr Hale's house was empty. Nobody saw him leave. The mayor said Mr Hale moved away. Nobody asked where. The next night, 15 rings. The baker was gone. Then 16. The whole Miller family. People stopped counting. They shut their curtains before midnight. Grandpa didn't. He sat by the fire with his stopwatch. "Count with me," he whispered. "If you stop counting, it counts you." Last night, the bell rang 30 times. I counted every single one. Grandpa's chair is empty now. Lily has his binoculars. She still sits at her window every night. But she doesn't watch the tower anymore. She watches me. And she's counting.
