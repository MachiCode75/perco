# The 3AM Neighbor

In this town, the power goes out at 3:00 every night. For exactly 11 minutes. Nobody looks outside. Lights out, eyes in. That's the rule. Every window in town has 3 curtains. Mr. Pike's hardware store sells 900 of them a year. The bakery opens at 3:11. Never 3:00. Our mayor gave every family a sleep mask that says NOT LOOKING. He wears 2. Every night, the whole town says it. "Lights out, eyes in." Like goodnight. My little sister Bea is 6. And she's not scared of anything. Every night, she leaves a cookie on her windowsill. "For the one who turns the lights off." Dad checks all 7 curtains twice. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. Mom hums the same song for 11 minutes. In the dark. And this year, I'm 10. So I close Bea's curtains now. Twice. Like Dad. Then I started noticing things. Every morning, Bea's cookie was gone. Mom said birds. The window was locked. There was black fur on the power pole. Way up. Higher than our roof. In the mud, there were footprints as long as Dad's car. One in front of every house. 23 houses. 23 footprints. At the library, I found a newspaper from 1994. 9 people gone in one winter. All from houses with the lights on. The blackouts started the next year. Bea asked me who turns the lights off. I said nobody. She said, "Then who eats the cookies?" So that night, at 3:00, I opened my curtain. 1 inch. Something was walking down our street. Taller than the power poles. Black fur. A grin as wide as a car. It stopped at the Millers' house, leaned down, and tapped their window. Tap. Tap. Tap. 3 Millers. Then the mayor's house. Tap. He lives alone. With his masks. Then it stopped at our house. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. 4. Mom, Dad, Bea, me. It was counting us. Then it turned to my window. And it smiled. The next morning, there was a note on our door. "You looked." Mom read it twice. Dad nailed my curtain shut. "Lights out, eyes in," he said. The next night, the lights went out at 2:59. One minute early. Then something knocked. Not on the door. On the roof. Then I heard Bea's window open. She had her flashlight. And a cookie. Click. The only light in the whole town. At the end of the street, in the trees, something opened its eyes. Lots of eyes. And they started coming toward the light. I ran down the hall, into her room, and grabbed the flashlight. Click. Dark. But the eyes kept coming. Closer. Then the ground shook. Thud. Thud. Thud. The giant stepped over our house and stood between us and the trees. It spread its arms wide. The eyes stopped. Then, one by one, they closed. The giant was never hunting us. It was hiding us. That's why the lights go out at 3:00. The things in the trees can only find us by our light. That's why it checks every window. One light is enough. That's why it counts us, every house, every night. Nobody's gone missing since 1994. And that's why the cookies were gone. Bea held out her cookie. The giant bent down, all the way down, and took it with 2 fingers. Very, very carefully. Then it tapped her window. Once. Bea tapped back. The next morning, there was a new note. "Thank you for the cookie." "Lights out, eyes in." Last night, the power went out at 3:00. Like always. The giant walked down our street, checking every house. It stopped at ours. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. There are only 4 of us.
